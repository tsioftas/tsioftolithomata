    Potami village (Miocene)

    Age
    11.61–5.33 million years ago
    Period
    Late Miocene
    Formation
    Pakhna formation (Koronia Member)
    Depositional environment
    Shallow marine reef. Clear, well-lit trobical-subtropical waters.
    Fossil types
    Bivalves, gastropods, corals, echinoids and calcareous algae
    Paleoecology highlights
    Reef-associated shallow-marine community, with corals and calcareous algae buildups and abundant molluscs and echinoids living around them. The fragmented shells indicate at least some wave/current reworking.

    During the Miocene, the area near modern Potami lay beneath a warm, shallow sea, where carbonate-rich sediments accumulated in a reef-associated environment. Corals and calcareous algae formed reef-like buildups across the seafloor, providing habitat for abundant bivalves, gastropods, echinoids, and other marine organisms. Waves and currents periodically broke and reworked shells and other skeletal remains, producing the fossil-rich bioclastic limestone preserved today.
