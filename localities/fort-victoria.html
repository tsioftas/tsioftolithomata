    🌳Tree of Life

    Fort Victoria, Isle of Wight

    • Age: ~32 million years ago
    • Period: Late Eocene (Priabonian)
    • Formation: Solent Group / Bembridge Limestone Formation
    • Depositional environment: Shallow coastal lagoon, swampy back‐areas, estuarine channels, with incursions from shallow sea
    • Fossil types: Crocodilian remains, turtle shells and fragments, plant debris, molluscs, swamp flora (e.g. mangrove-style vegetation, swamp palms, ferns)
    • Paleoecology highlights: Tropical coastal wetland margin. Shallow lagoon, swamp forests, marsh channels winding through saturated ground. Occasional tidal influence. Periods of subaerial exposure and small freshwater influxes. Accumulation of organic matter, roots, and leaf litter in swamp hollows.

    Imagine a broad, shallow lagoon stretching inland from a low coast. The water is calm and greenish, shallow enough to show muddy bottoms in places. Along the water’s edge, swampy ground with soggy soils, where stilted roots of palms, mangrove-type trees, ferns, and marsh grasses grow. Channels sinuously cut through the swamp, carrying weak currents and occasional influxes of brackish water. In places, low muddy islands or levees rise slightly above water, with denser vegetation clumps. The horizon is low, warm sunlight overhead; occasional glimpses of more open water beyond or tidal flats.
