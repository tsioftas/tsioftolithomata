    Tree of Life

    Cape Aspro, Pissouri

    Age
    5.33–2.58 million years ago
    Period
    Pliocene
    Formation
    Lefkosia Formation
    Depositional environment
    Open marine shelf
    Fossil types
    Gastropods, bivalves, corals, marine vertebrates
    Paleoecology highlights
    Warm temperate waters, high sea level, coral patches, well-oxygenated surface waters, occasional bottom-water anoxia

    Imagine warm, shallow waters stretching over what is now the sun-bleached cliffs of Cape Aspro. The sea glows in hues of turquoise and pale blue, gently rippling above a soft, marl-covered seafloor. In the clear depths, colonies of corals anchor themselves to firm patches, forming scattered reef mounds that break up the gentle plain. Between them, beds of fine calcareous mud settle quietly, rich with drifting plankton and microscopic shells. Occasional layers of still, oxygen-starved water leave streaks of dark sapropel in the sediment. The waters above shimmer with sunlight toward the surface, alive with swarms of plankton and all sorts of pelagic marine life.
