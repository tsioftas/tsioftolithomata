# PR #218: new taxon text and images

Audited 7 September 2026. Scope: the three new taxonomy entries, including names, keywords, descriptions and etymologies in English, Modern Greek, Koine-style Greek and Cypriot Greek. Descriptions stay at three short paragraphs for general readers. The existing bacteria language-key correction is valid; its pre-existing scientific prose and the specimen identifications are outside this text audit.

## Findings and decisions

- **Sawskate anatomy:** removed denticles “set in sockets”, which confuses their attachment with living sawfish. Retained replacement without claiming it occurs only after wear or breakage. Removed sediment-digging and shoal-striking as established behaviours. [Welten et al. (2015), developmental and fossil comparison](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4593678/); [Underwood et al. (2016), Sclerorhynchus and convergence](https://doi.org/10.1144/SP430.7).
- **Fossil record:** included oral teeth as well as rostral denticles; retained Cretaceous age and end-Cretaceous extinction. The page illustrates a saw-bearing member rather than claiming every member of every modern circumscription had a saw. [Kriwet (2004), systematic revision](https://www.pfeil-verlag.de/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/3_53d03.pdf).
- **Classification:** preserved this PR's explicitly chosen PBDB-based keys and hierarchy. The prose identifies Sclerorhynchidae as traditional broad usage and explains that newer research separates Onchopristis into Onchopristidae. Sclerorhynchiformes is Kriwet's 2004 order; Sclerorhynchoidei is used in newer classifications. These are alternative circumscriptions, not interchangeable assertions of universal consensus.
- **Family authorship:** removed “Cappetta established the family in 1974”. Greenfield attributes Sclerorhynchidae to Arambourg (1952), under coordination from Sclerorhynchinae, and identifies priority of Ganopristidae Arambourg (1940) when the type genera are synonymized. An eventual migration to the newer hierarchy should address both family names and specimen assignments together. [Greenfield (2021), nomenclatural correction](https://doi.org/10.11646/bionomina.22.1.3).
- **Onchopristis:** removed “principal family”, “largest members”, unsupported usual breakage claims and family-wide use of a genus-level barb character. Replaced “enamelled” with enameloid, the actual tissue. [Chateauneuf-Gautier et al., enameloid study](https://doi.org/10.1111/joa.70215).
- **Pterosaurs:** clarified that birds survived the dinosaur extinction and that pterosaurs are a separate related group. Retained powered flight, the fourth wing finger and hollow bones. Replaced the blanket “large fish-eaters” with toothed and toothless diversity and qualified diets. [NHM overview](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/the-truth-about-pterosaurs.html); [AMNH wing anatomy](https://www.amnh.org/exhibitions/permanent/vertebrate-origins/pterosaurs); [Smith et al. (2023), Kem Kem pterosaur review](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12542-022-00642-6); [Bestwick et al. (2018), dietary evidence review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6849529/).
- **Etymologies:** retained meaningful Greek roots and shortened naming history. The barb sense of ὄγκος is supported by [Perseus dictionaries](https://atlas.perseus.tufts.edu/dictionaries/headword/ὄγκος/). Kaup's 1834 attribution is retained in [Codorniú et al. (2016)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5012331/).

## Languages

All four versions communicate the same substantive claims and uncertainty. Modern Greek replaces “σμαλτωμένη” with an explanation of enameloid. Koine-style prose avoids the modern loan “ἐσμαλτωμένη”, simplifies strained syntax and uses Latin taxon labels where inflection would obscure identification. Modern taxonomic and geological concepts necessarily require later technical vocabulary: this is a Koine-style rendering, not an ancient attested text. Cypriot descriptions and etymologies were missing and are now filled, using the site's τζ̌αι, έσ̌ει, ψάρκα and related spelling conventions. Keywords use searchable scientific Greek terms. This is an editorial translation review, not independent certification by a dialect specialist.

## Images

Built-in image generation was used for three new illustrations in the site's cream-paper natural-history style. These are illustrative reconstructions, not photographs or new morphological evidence. Body colours and soft tissues are interpretive. The order plate depicts Sclerorhynchus, Libanopristis, Onchopristis and Ischyrhiza; the family plate depicts Sclerorhynchus, Libanopristis and Micropristis. These are illustrative reconstructions, not diagnostic species portraits or re-identifications of collection specimens. The family plate focuses on its core genera. See the [morphological phylogeny review](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-2818/14/6/456) and [sclerorhynchid review](https://www.andeangeology.cl/index.php/revista1/article/view/V31n1-a05/html). The pterosaur plate follows the six-member selection in the shared chat: Anurognathus, Rhamphorhynchus, Pterodaustro, Tapejara, Pteranodon and Quetzalcoatlus. It shows group diversity, not a Kem Kem assemblage or a scale comparison. The replacements use the existing Sauropsida and Dasyatidae plates as style references. Visual review prompted corrections to a duplicated dorsal fin and the placement of Pterodaustro filtering teeth.

Files under `images/thumbnails/`: `Σκληρορυγχόμορφα.png`, `Σκληρορυγχίδες.png`, `Πτεροσαύρια.png`, with matching JPEG, `webp_dir/*.webp` and `thumbs_dir/*_thumb.{jpg,webp}` derivatives. Prompts are recorded in `pr-218-image-prompts.json`. The existing generator selects these names automatically; no template changes are needed.
