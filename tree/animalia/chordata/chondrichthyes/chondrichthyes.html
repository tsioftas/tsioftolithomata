    Class Chondrichthyes

    Chondrichthyes, the cartilaginous fishes, are a class of jawed vertebrates characterized by a skeleton made entirely of cartilage, rather than bone. They possess paired fins, gills for breathing, and typically have a streamlined body shape. This group includes sharks, rays, and chimaeras, showcasing a diverse range of adaptations for marine life, from apex predators to bottom-dwellers.

    Etymology

    “Chondrichthyes” from Greek χόνδρος (chóndros, “cartilage”) + ἰχθύς (ichthýs, “fish”), i.e. “cartilaginous fish” — referring to their cartilaginous rather than bony skeleton. Established as a taxonomic name by the English biologist Thomas Henry Huxley in 1880.
