    Turritellidae, or turret shells, are a family of marine gastropods known for their tall, tightly coiled shells that resemble small screws. Unlike most Caenogastropoda, which are often predators or grazers, turritellids are suspension feeders, filtering particles from water using cilia. Found worldwide in marine sediments, they have an extensive fossil record dating back to the Early Cretaceous. Their unique shell shape and feeding strategy set them apart from other gastropods.

    Etymology

    “Turritellidae” from the type genus Turritella, a Latin diminutive of turris (“tower”), i.e. “little tower” — referring to the tall, slender, tightly coiled shell shaped like a small conical tower. The family was established by Lovén in 1847; the genus Turritella by Lamarck in 1799.
