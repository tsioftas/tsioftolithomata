    Venericor planicosta is a large, heavy-shelled carditid and one of the most conspicuous bivalves of the European Eocene. Its rounded to obliquely oval valves may approach ten centimetres in length and bear about twenty to thirty broad, low radial ribs separated by narrow grooves. It lived in dense populations on warm, shallow shelf bottoms, probably partly buried with the shell margin near the sediment surface. Its valves are abundant in the Bracklesham Group of southern England, including the interval traditionally called the Cardita Bed, and in coeval deposits of the Paris and Belgian basins. The species is still often published as Venericardia planicosta or Venericardia (Venericor) planicosta.

    Etymology

    The epithet planicosta combines Latin planus (“flat”) and costa (“rib”), describing the shell's broad, flattened radial ribs. Lamarck named Venericardia planicosta in 1801; the accepted combination Venericor planicosta is generally credited to Le Renard's 1994 treatment.
