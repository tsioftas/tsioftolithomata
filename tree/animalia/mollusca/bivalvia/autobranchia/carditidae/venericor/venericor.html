    Genus †Venericor

    Venericor is an extinct Paleogene genus of large carditid bivalves distinguished by thick, strongly inflated valves and broad, smooth or weakly ornamented radial ribs that flatten as the shell grows. This planicostate shell form separates it from the more sharply ribbed carditids historically grouped with it in Venericardia. Venericor species inhabited warm, shallow seas and commonly formed dense populations whose heavy valves accumulated in shell beds. The genus is especially diverse in Paleocene and Eocene deposits of Europe and the Americas. It has often been treated as a subgenus of Venericardia, but morphological phylogenetic analysis supports the planicostate Venericor lineage as a coherent group.

    Etymology

    “Venericor” combines the stem Veneri-, referring to Venus, with Latin cor (“heart”), describing the heart-like form of the paired valves. Ralph Bentley Stewart established the genus in 1930; it is also frequently treated as Venericardia (Venericor).
