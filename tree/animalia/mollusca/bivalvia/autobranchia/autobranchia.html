    Autobranchia are a more derived group of bivalves, differing from Protobranchia mainly in their gills. Their gills are enlarged and specialized, serving both for breathing and for capturing food from the water current. They possess inhalant and exhalant siphons, which Protobranchs lack. This group includes familiar forms such as mussels, scallops, and oysters.

    Etymology

    “Autobranchia” from Greek αὐτο- (auto-, “self, own”) + βράγχια (bránchia, “gills”), referring to their distinctive, self-sufficient gills — large, lamellar, dual-purpose for both respiration and filter-feeding, unlike the simpler gills of other bivalves. The subclass was established by the Austrian zoologist Karl Grobben in 1894.
